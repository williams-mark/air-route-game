Scope: the whole globe (lane/globe-data, see "Globe expansion"), starting from the United States (49 states and DC; Delaware had no scheduled service in 2015) and Europe (EU27, UK, Ireland, Iceland, Norway, Switzerland, Western Balkans), campaign 2015-2025. The original 60 California, Hawaii, Oregon and Washington rows are described in the first sections; the world expansion (lane/world-data) is in "World expansion" below. Confidence legend: SOURCED = taken from the named dataset by script; RECALLED = figure from published manufacturer/Wikipedia spec tables entered from memory, not re-fetched in this session (spot-check before treating as authoritative); ESTIMATE = reasoned approximation, not a published figure.

## Overwater / ETOPS model (src/overwater.ts, `maxOverwaterKm`; src/geo.ts, `canFlyRoute`)

`canFlyRoute(type, a, b)` = within usable range AND runway at both ends >= runwayNeededM x 0.85 AND, if the route is overwater, distance <= `maxOverwaterKm[type.id]`. A route is overwater if the endpoints are in different regions (CA/HI) or are Hawaii airports on different islands (island mapping hard-coded in geo.ts). It ignores introYear/endYear, payload and slots.

`maxOverwaterKm` = 2 x (diversion minutes / 60) x 0.9 x cruiseKmh: the route midpoint may be at most one diversion radius from either end, assuming no usable alternate mid-ocean. The 0.9 one-engine-out speed fraction is an ESTIMATE. Diversion minutes by family:
- 180: 737 NG and MAX, A319/A320/A321 ceo and neo, A220-100/300, 757-200, 767-300ER. Sourced: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETOPS (Aloha's 180-minute-approved 737-700s flew the Hawaii-West Coast routes incl. Honolulu-Burbank; the 757, 767 and A320 are ETOPS-approved but no rating is given). A220: ETOPS 180 from Transport Canada, Jan 2019 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A220). The 180 figure for the MAX and A320 family is an ASSUMPTION (same class as NG and A220); it was not read from a certification document.
- 330 for 777 (ETOPS-330 approved Dec 2011) and 787 (ETOPS-330, May 2014); 240 for A330 (first ETOPS-240, 2009); 370 for A350 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETOPS). These make widebody twins effectively unlimited for CA-HI.
- Unlimited: B747-400 (four engines).
- 60: E175, CRJ700/900, ATR72-600. Basis: the US 60-minute rule for twins without ETOPS approval (ETOPS page). These types are not documented as ETOPS-approved in the sources read; this is an ASSUMPTION. The resulting 918-1,490 km limit lets them fly all inter-island hops (longest in the dataset, LIH-ITO, is 511 km) but not CA-HI.

Resulting limits: 737/A320 family 4,536 km, A220 4,471 km, 757 4,633 km, 767 4,590 km. LAX-HNL is 4,108 km and SAN-HNL 4,201 km; the longest CA-HI pair in the dataset is IPL-LIH at 4,452 km, so all narrowbody twins with enough range can reach Hawaii from every CA airport. 

A220 and LAX-HNL: the requested rule (A220-100 cannot do LAX-HNL) is NOT applied because the facts found point the other way: A220 has ETOPS 180 (Transport Canada, Jan 2019), a published range of 6,300 km (A220-100) against a 4,108 km sector, and Airbus's own announcement cited Honolulu-Los Angeles as an example route (https://economyclassandbeyond.boardingarea.com/2019/01/14/airbus-a220-gains-etops180-certification-opening-up-some-very-intresting-city-pairs ; Airbus press release itself not readable). I found no source for an operator-practice ban, and did not verify whether any airline ever flew an A220 to Hawaii. The test therefore asserts A220 CAN fly LAX-HNL and LAX-HNL stays range-feasible for A220 (if you want a gameplay restriction, set a lower `diversionMinutes` for A220 in overwater.ts and say so here).

### Inclusion rule and evidence
Rule: scheduled commercial passenger service in 2015 and about 250,000 or more passengers that year, plus a few remote or sole-service airports (flag S in the table: ANC FAI JNU KTN SIT BET OME OTZ BRW ADQ in Alaska; KOI LSI SYY Scottish islands; RNN Bornholm; VBY Gotland; RKV and AEY Iceland domestic hubs). The "pax 2015 (k, recalled)" column of the table is the evidence (thousands of passengers, recalled; it is not an Airport field). Airports below the threshold in 2015 are not in the data even if they have service today. Some borderline rows (200-300k) are judgement calls. Candidates dropped for lack of a sourced runway length: none (every airport has an OurAirports runway). ### Sourced fields
- id, lat, lon, name: OurAirports `airports.csv` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/davidmegginson/ourairports-data/main/airports.csv, public domain, snapshot fetched 2026-11-03). name is OurAirports' name shortened by `clean_name` plus a few overrides; city is OurAirports' municipality or an override.
- runwayM: OurAirports `runways.csv`, longest non-closed runway in feet x 0.3048 (closed runways allowed for flag X airports). Reflects today's runways, not 2015 (e.g. a few airports lengthened or shortened since).
- country: OurAirports `iso_country` (UK is GB). region: US state code (DC for the District of Columbia) or the ISO country code outside the US.

### Landmass convention
`landmass` decides land pair vs overwater (geo.ts). One string per connected piece of land: `US-CONUS`, `US-AK`, `US-HI` (Hawaii: per-island rule in geo.ts, as before); `EU-MAINLAND` = continental Europe including Scandinavia, Finland, the Baltics, the Balkans and the Danish islands joined by fixed links (Zealand, Funen: Great Belt and Oresund bridges); `GB` = Great Britain; `IE` = the island of Ireland (Republic and Northern Ireland, so BFS and BHD are country GB, landmass IE); `IS` Iceland; `CY` Cyprus; `MT` Malta; `JE` `GG` `IM` Crown Dependencies; every other island with an airport is `<ISO country>-<Island>`: GB-Orkney, GB-Shetland, GB-Hebrides, FR-Corsica, ES-Mallorca, ES-Ibiza, ES-Menorca, ES-Tenerife (TFS and TFN), ES-GranCanaria, ES-Lanzarote, ES-Fuerteventura, ES-LaPalma, IT-Sicily, IT-Sardinia, GR-Crete, GR-Rhodes, GR-Corfu, GR-Kos, GR-Santorini, GR-Zakynthos, GR-Mykonos, GR-Kefalonia, GR-Lesbos, GR-Samos, GR-Skiathos, PT-Madeira, DK-Bornholm, SE-Gotland. Archipelagos are split per island because islands have no road link. Known simplification: Alaska is one landmass although most of it is roadless (the sim's ground-alternative damping will wrongly apply to pairs such as ANC-NOME); a finer split was not made to keep the convention simple. CONUS<->AK is overwater although the great circle crosses Canada.

### Estimated fields (metroPopulation, gdpPerCapita, tourismIndex, slotsPerHour)
All four are ESTIMATES. Anchored = a value in the TSV (the "metroPop"/"GDP/cap" columns of the evidence table say "recalled"): approximate metropolitan population (US: MSA or CSA as in the original rows, e.g. the whole NYC MSA for JFK, LGA, EWR, HPN and ISP, the Washington MSA for IAD and DCA and the Baltimore MSA for BWI; Europe: urban area or functional urban area, e.g. London 11.5M for all six London airports) and GDP per head in USD of the metro, recalled, not fetched. Anchors exist for the ~50 largest US metros and ~50 largest European metros and for many small places where I recalled a population (marked "recalled" for pop only; GDP then comes from the formula). Rows marked "formula" use the deterministic formulas below; everything is reproducible from the TSVs and the script.

Formulas (P = 2015 passengers in millions from the TSV; constants in the script):
- metroPopulation = clamp(round_to_5000(600,000 x P^0.75), 20,000, 25,000,000) when no anchor. (Calibration: 75 M pax -> 15 M, 10 M -> 3.4 M, 1 M -> 0.6 M, 0.3 M -> 240 k.)
- gdpPerCapita = round_to_1000(COUNTRY_GDP[country] x clamp(0.85 + 0.20 x log10(pop / 1,000,000), 0.80, 1.35)) when no anchor.
- tourismIndex = clamp(round_to_0.05(COUNTRY_TOUR[country] + 0.35 if flag R (resort/leisure destination) + 0.25 if flag C (major tourist city)), 0.05, 0.9). COUNTRY_TOUR is a judgement table in the script (US 0.15, GB 0.25, FR 0.40, DE 0.20, IT 0.50, ES 0.55, GR 0.55, HR 0.55, CY 0.55, MT 0.60, PT 0.45, IS 0.45, AT 0.35, CH 0.30, Nordics 0.15-0.20, Eastern Europe 0.15-0.30, Balkans 0.10-0.45). Flags R and C are my judgement per airport.
- slotsPerHour = clamp(round(11 x sqrt(P)), 4, 120). (LHR 75 M -> 95, 10 M -> 35, 1 M -> 11; ordinary movements per hour an airport can allocate, not a published slot cap.)

### Geography rules (src/geo.ts, src/overwater.ts)
- `isLandPair(a, b)` = same `landmass`, except Hawaii (never a land pair, as before). `isOverwaterRoute(a, b)` = different landmass, or HI-HI on different islands. CA, OR and WA are all `US-CONUS`, so behaviour for the original 60 airports is unchanged (tests unchanged and passing).
- Overwater permission: `canFlyOverwater(type, a, b)` is true if km <= `maxOverwaterKm[type]` (2 x ETOPS radius, as before: a route with no alternate) OR the whole great circle (sampled every 100 km) lies within the type's ETOPS radius `etopsRadiusKm[type]` = diversion minutes/60 x 0.9 x cruise km/h of the end airports, one of `OCEANIC_ALTERNATES` (Keflavik, Kangerlussuaq, Narsarsuaq, Gander, Goose Bay, St. John's, Halifax, Iqaluit, Shannon, Prestwick, Vagar, Lajes, Santa Maria; OurAirports coordinates; usable as alternates by public aviation knowledge, assumed always available) or any playable airport with a runway of 2,000 m or more. The second clause only adds routes; it is what lets 180-minute narrowbodies fly the northern North Atlantic via Newfoundland, Greenland, Iceland and Ireland as in real operations (ETOPS-180 is the standard basis for 757/767/A321/737 North Atlantic flying) and 60-minute regional types fly along coasts and between islands but not across the ocean. Diversion minutes per type (180 narrowbodies, 240 A330, 330 777/787, 370 A350, 60 regionals, none for the 747) are the existing assumptions documented under "Overwater / ETOPS model" above and are unchanged.
- Resulting behaviour (tests): JFK-LHR (5,540 km) is overwater and flown by B787-8/9/10, B777-200ER/300ER, A330, A350, B747, B767-300ER and B757-200; the A321N (usable range 5,525 km with the 0.85 factor), 737 MAX 8 and A220 cannot (range) but all fly BOS-DUB (4,804 km), JFK-SNN, and BOS/JFK-KEF. No dedicated A321LR type exists in aircraft.ts (not invented), so JFK-LHR misses A321N by 15 km. MIA-MAD (7,106 km) and ORD-CDG are widebody-only (range). E175, CRJ, ATR cannot cross the Atlantic (range) but E175 flies MAD-TFS (Canary Islands) and KEF-EDI because alternates are always within 60 minutes. Remaining permitted/blocked cases that the radius rule decides are mostly CONUS-AK routes over Canada (E175 JNU-MSP blocked).
- Caveat: alternate availability is a planning assumption. The ETOPS radius is a straight-line approximation (no winds, 0.9 one-engine-out speed fraction estimated); payload, weather, slots and runway elevation are ignored as before.

## Globe expansion (lane/globe-data, added 2026-11-06)

508 airports added to the US and Europe set (1,006 in all): Canada, Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean, South America, Africa, the Middle East, Turkey, the Caucasus and Central Asia, South, East and Southeast Asia, Australia, New Zealand and the Pacific islands (Hawaii and Alaska were already present). Input lists: `scripts/world_airports_amer.tsv` (Americas), `world_airports_afme.tsv` (Africa, Middle East, Central Asia), `world_airports_asoc.tsv` (Asia, Oceania), same pipe format and generator as the World expansion above; the evidence table above covers the new rows too.
- Sourced (OurAirports, public domain, `airports.csv` / `runways.csv` fetched 2026-11-06 from the davidmegginson/ourairports-data mirror): id (IATA, matched to exactly one OurAirports record or an explicit `ident` override), name (shortened), lat, lon, country, runwayM (longest non-closed runway, ft x 0.3048; the file reflects today's runways, so a few differ from 2015).
- Inclusion rule (judgement, not a dataset): airports with roughly 1 million or more passengers a year in 2015-2019 where known, every national hub or capital airport, and notable island and tourism airports, with a few remote sole-service airports (flag S). The "pax" column is a RECALLED, rounded 2015-era figure from memory (not re-fetched) and is used only to pick the airport and to size the estimates; spot-check before relying on it. Several rows are weaker recollections (e.g. smaller Chinese, Indian, Brazilian and Pacific airports).
- Estimated, as in the World expansion: metroPopulation (recalled metro anchors for large cities, `600000 x pax^0.75` otherwise), gdpPerCapita (recalled 2015 country GDP per head, rounded, times a city-size factor), tourismIndex (country baseline plus 0.35 for resort and 0.25 for tourist-city flags), slotsPerHour (`11 x sqrt(pax)`). All four are ESTIMATES. The country GDP and tourism tables for the new countries are in the generator (`COUNTRY_GDP`, `COUNTRY_TOUR`); Venezuela, Cuba, Iran, Iraq, Afghanistan, Sudan and some small states are low-confidence.
- 2015 identity quirks: IST is Istanbul Atatürk (LTBA; the new Istanbul airport took the code in 2019), DKR is the old Dakar Léopold Sédar Senghor (GOOY, closed 2017, flag X), ISB is the old Islamabad airport (OPRN, closed 2018, flag X), REP is the old Siem Reap airport (KH-0003, closed 2023, flag X), FRU is Bishkek Manas (UAFM, which OurAirports now codes BSZ). Airports opened after 2015 that matter (new Istanbul, Beijing Daxing, Mexico City NAIM, Western Sydney) are not in the data. No closure or opening calendar is added for these (the sim lane owns world/closures.ts).
- Landmass convention for the new regions: `CA`, `MX`, `NA-CENTRAL` (Guatemala to Panama), `SA-MAINLAND`, `AF-MAINLAND`, `AS-MAINLAND` (Turkey, the Middle East, Central and South Asia, mainland China, Korea, mainland Southeast Asia, Peninsular Malaysia, Singapore, Hong Kong, Macau), `AU`; Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu share `JP-Honshu` (bridges, tunnels, Shinkansen); every other island or island group with an airport is `<ISO>-<Island>` (Hokkaido, Okinawa, Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica, Puerto Rico, Cebu, Luzon, Java, Bali, Borneo, Tasmania, NZ North and South, Viti Levu, Tahiti, ...) or just the ISO code for single-island countries (LK, TW, MV, CU, JM, PR, BB, ...). Sri Lanka, Taiwan, Bahrain (causeway ignored), Hong Kong (bridge) and Singapore (causeway) follow that rule; Singapore and Hong Kong are on the mainland landmass.
- geo.ts: `isOverwaterRoute` now also treats landmasses joined by land as one block (`LAND_CONNECTED`: US-CONUS, CA, MX, NA-CENTRAL; and EU-MAINLAND, AS-MAINLAND, AF-MAINLAND via Turkey, the Caucasus and Sinai), so JFK-YYZ or IST-ATH are not overwater. `isLandPair` is unchanged (same `landmass`), so the sim's surface-alternative model sees no road or rail link across those borders. Hawaii, Alaska, Great Britain, Ireland and islands remain overwater.
- Alternates: every new airport with a runway of 2,000 m or more joins the ETOPS alternate set automatically (overwater.ts `ALTERNATE_MIN_RUNWAY_M`), which makes the Pacific, Tasman, Caribbean and Canada routes more permissive than before (e.g. an E175 can now fly Juneau-Minneapolis over the Canadian airports).
- Metro groups added (tuning weights, not statistics): Toronto, Sao Paulo, Rio, Buenos Aires, Istanbul, Tehran, Dubai-Sharjah, Tokyo, Osaka-Kobe, Seoul, Taipei, Shanghai, Bangkok, Jakarta.

### Start year (src/time/startYear.ts, src/time/calendar.ts)
- MIN_START_YEAR = 1965 is the policy floor the spec asked for. A year is also rejected when no aircraft type is in production that year, because the starting fleet is built from types in production. The earliest year with a fleet is the first introduction year in the data (1972 with the DC-10-30). The error text names it.
- Economic curves are still the 2015-2025 calibration: the Jet-A series (finance/fuel.ts) and carbon anchors (world/regions.ts) clamp to their first value before 2015, so pre-2015 fuel is 1.8 USD/gal, not the historical price. No pre-2015 fuel series is sourced.
- Demand growth (demand/market.ts, 2.5% a year from 2015) compounds backward before 2015 and forward after it.
- Airport opening and closing dates are absolute (world/closures.ts), so a 1972 start has the 2015 airports open at their 1972 status (closed airports only close later).
